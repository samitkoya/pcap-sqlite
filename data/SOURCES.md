# Capture provenance and label limits

All ten bundled files are public packet captures, parsed locally with PyShark and TShark. The detector did not generate or assign their source labels. The two main captures remain at `data/normal.pcap` and `data/tunneling.pcap` for the approved course workflow; eight comparison captures are in `data/additional/`.

The exact primary tunnel archive is [8c2ef973a2d27a2c4581f347aa196b5b_full_dataset.tar.gz](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Korving-F/dns-tunnel-dataset/master/dns_tunnel_c2/8c2ef973a2d27a2c4581f347aa196b5b/8c2ef973a2d27a2c4581f347aa196b5b_full_dataset.tar.gz); the copied member is `8c2ef973a2d27a2c4581f347aa196b5b/bind9/dns.pcap`. Its accompanying scenario metadata and BIND log identify DNSCAT C2 activity involving `example.attack`. The [dmachard repository README](https://github.com/dmachard/datasets-malicious-dns) identifies the tools used to generate its five captures, but does not provide packet-level labels or a separate benign comparison set. These are controlled tool examples, not proof of live malicious incidents.

The observed domain name in a capture is not by itself a ground-truth label. The general Wireshark capture cannot support a false-positive-rate claim. The tool captures can test whether these particular scenarios are flagged, but their small size and shared source cannot support a population-level accuracy claim. The iodine example has 12 NULL queries, `txt_null_ratio=1.0`, `avg_entropy=3.300`, `max_entropy=4.815`, and `avg_query_len=40.667`. The original mean-only entropy rule scored it 1/4. The revised entropy rule awards its entropy point for maximum > 4.5 when TXT/NULL ratio > 0.3, making it 2/4 at the unchanged default threshold. This rule was selected after observing iodine; this same sample cannot independently validate its broader performance.

The CTU normal-activity capture produced 112 domain summaries, all below the revised 2/4 flag threshold. This is useful comparison traffic, but the source-level normal description does not certify every domain. The DNS2TCP KEY capture produced two summaries, with `example.attack` at 2/4. The DNS2TCP TXT capture produced four summaries, with `example.attack` and `powerdns.com` at 3/4. All three `powerdns.com` queries have the same TXT name, `recursor-4.2.1-1build2.Ubuntu.security-status.secpoll.powerdns.com`. [PowerDNS documents](https://doc.powerdns.com/recursor/security.html) this `recursor-...security-status.secpoll.powerdns.com` TXT pattern as its software security-status polling. That is independent evidence for a routine explanation of these queries, but it does not certify this capture's domain as benign or provide a capture-specific label. The dashboard marks its ground truth unverified, and `detector.py --known-malicious example.attack` reports it as an unverified flag rather than a false positive. User uploads are stored separately under `data/uploads/` and `output/uploads/`, are ignored by Git, and have no ground-truth label unless independently documented.
